Meaning & tradition

The armor of the Goddess.

A prayer that places the whole of life under the care of Devī: the body, the senses, our relationships, and the paths we take.

What is the Devi Kavacham?

Devī means Goddess; kavaca means armor. In this hymn, Mārkaṇḍeya asks Brahmā for a teaching of protection. Brahmā responds by naming the forms of the Goddess and invoking their care.

The opening dedication places the prayer within the Saptaśatī recitation. Near the end, verse 53 explicitly says to don this armor before reciting the Caṇḍī Saptaśatī. This makes the Kavacham a preparation for the larger devotional reading.

What does the prayer do?

Its central act is invocation: asking the Goddess to guard each part of the devotee’s world. The prayer begins with her names, surrounds the devotee with directional guardians, and then moves through the body, breath, senses, family, and journeys.

As a way of reading it, this movement can be understood as gathering attention and entrusting one’s whole life to the Divine Mother. The concluding verses describe protection, prosperity, and liberation in the hymn’s devotional language.

Why is it important?

The Kavacham gives a concrete form to devotion: even speech, fingers, footsteps, and the places one has forgotten to name are included in the prayer. Its many goddess names make protection feel present throughout ordinary life.

In the broader tradition of Mother worship, Belur Math describes the Divine Mother as a source of care, nourishment, and protection, and the Caṇḍī as a valued scripture of that tradition. The Kavacham’s own invitations to refuge and fearlessness belong within this devotional setting. Read Belur Math’s introduction to Mother worship.

Many names, one unfolding prayer.

The nine Durgās appear early in the hymn. They are followed by the Mothers and many other forms of Devī. Rather than trying to memorize every name at once, follow the prayer’s movement: who is being invoked, and what is being entrusted to her care?

About this edition

This English reading edition was created in honor of Durga Puja. It preserves the supplied 56-verse text, including its wording and diacritics. The explanations here are a reading companion; they are separate from the hymn itself.

Verse numbers refer to this edition. Green and black ink alternate to help distinguish verses; the colors do not assign different meanings or importance.
